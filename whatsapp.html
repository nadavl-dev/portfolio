WhatsApp Messaging
at Scale

How I turned a silent budget leak into a reliable delivery system — saving Bites over $10,000 a year while getting more content into the hands of frontline workers.

Bites Product ownership WhatsApp Business Platform $10,000+ saved / year
The problem

WhatsApp is how Bites reaches frontline workers — it is the primary channel for delivering training and communication to people who don't sit behind a desk. If a message fails, the content simply never arrives.

Two things were going wrong at once. Failed sends meant lost reach: workers never received their training, and nobody knew until engagement numbers dropped. And we were still paying for it: every send costs money on the WhatsApp Business Platform, including retries that were blindly re-sent into the same failure.

The cost was buried in an infrastructure bill nobody read line by line. I found it because clients kept asking the same question: "Why didn't my team get the Bite?"

My approach

I started from the data, not the tooling. I mapped where deliveries were failing, which message types failed most, and what a failed send actually cost us across a year. That turned a vague "sometimes messages don't arrive" complaint into a quantified, prioritized problem.

  • Audited the full message flow — from trigger to delivery receipt — to find where sends silently died.
  • Quantified the waste — repeated blind retries were effectively doubling the cost of every failed conversation.
  • Designed for the failure case first — the system had to know what to do when a message doesn't land, not just when it does.
What I built

I built a two-part system on the WhatsApp Business Platform:

  • Approved message templates — properly structured WhatsApp Business templates that pass Meta's approval flow, so messages send reliably at scale instead of getting throttled or rejected.
  • Automatic fallback routing — when a delivery fails, the system reroutes to an alternative channel automatically instead of paying for blind retries into the same dead end.

The design principle was simple: every send should either arrive or trigger a smarter next step — never a silent failure, never a wasted retry.

The impact

The results were measurable on both sides of the problem — cost and reach:

$10,000+ saved annually in messaging spend by eliminating blind retries
↑ higher message-receiving rate — more training actually reaching frontline workers
0 silent failures — every failed send now triggers a fallback automatically

Beyond the numbers, this project is how I work: I found a recurring customer complaint, traced it to a quantifiable business problem, designed a system around the failure case, and shipped it — from inside a customer success role, without waiting for a roadmap slot.
